**Scope: Days AGO.** The legacy scheduler actors, including VirtualClock and the floating-point RED policy, are described in [Legacy schedulers](/docs/legacy/components/schedulers).

Schedulers combine queue admission, packet selection, and non-preemptive link service. Lowering gives each switch-egress LP one queue and one closed `SchedulerKind` state value.

## Service sequence

The executor represents service as three transitions:

1. `RemoteArrival` applies admission and inserts the packet.
2. `TxReady` selects one eligible packet and commits it to `in_service`.
3. `TxComplete` releases the server, updates discipline state, forwards the transmitted packet as a remote arrival, and schedules another local selection when work remains.

The event key gives arrivals phase 0, completions phase 1, and service selection phase 2 at equal nanosecond times. PFC eligibility is checked before selection, and service remains non-preemptive.

## Disciplines

| Config value | Executor state | Scalar/CPU | Metal/CUDA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `FIFO` | `Fifo` | yes | yes |
| `SP` | `StaticPriority` | yes | yes |
| `WFQ` | `WeightedFairQueue` | yes | yes |
| `DRR` | `DeficitRoundRobin` | yes | yes |
| `WRR` | `WeightedRoundRobin` | yes | yes |
| `VirtualClock` | none | rejected at compilation | rejected at compilation |

Classed schedulers use `flow_id % class_count`. SP orders larger configured priorities first. DRR stores byte quanta and deficits; each class quantum is `1500 * weight / smallest_weight` bytes. WRR stores per-class packet counts. WFQ stores virtual time and finish tags as reduced arbitrary-precision rationals on Scalar and CPU; Metal and CUDA use checked 320-bit rational components, and device validation requires each rational to fit.

Lowering rejects zero class weights. VirtualClock fails before image construction:

```text
unsupported scheduler `VirtualClock`; Days executor supports FIFO, SP, WFQ, DRR, and WRR
```

## Admission and ECN

| Policy | Scalar/CPU | Metal/CUDA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TailDrop at the packet capacity | yes | yes |
| ECN ramp (`switch.ecn`, `switch.ecn_by_rate`) | yes | yes |

A TailDrop queue drops a packet that would exceed its packet capacity; capacity zero means unlimited. An ECN queue drops a packet that would exceed its byte capacity (`ecn_capacity_bytes`) and marks ECN-capable data on the Kmin/Kmax/Pmax ramp over its byte depth after admission, with a stateless hash draw between the thresholds. The step is the ramp with `kmin = kmax` and `pmax = 1`. Packets that are not ECN-capable data (ACKs, NACKs, CNPs, PFC frames) are never marked and never draw. The rule, its exact integer form and the draw are on [Switches & Queues](/docs/configuration/switches).

The policy is immutable: no queue carries marking state, so checkpoints and device readback hold none. RED and RED_ECN were removed in P16; Days AGO refuses them, and `ECN_THRESHOLD`, at lowering.

## PFC queue state

Queues store their PFC monitors and pause-controller sets beside scheduler state. PFC runs on Scalar, CPU, Metal, and CUDA. See [PFC](/docs/components/l2) and [Switch configuration](/docs/configuration/switches).
