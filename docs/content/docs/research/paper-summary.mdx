Two research lines share this repository: **LeanGuard**, the trace-certificate
methodology built on the legacy engine, and the **exact executor** (Days AGO),
whose execution model is itself proved in Lean and whose backends are held to
byte-identity. This page summarizes both, and is careful to separate shipped
code, committed fixtures, and external campaign evidence.

## LeanGuard: trace certificates + trusted replay

![LeanGuard overview: the simulator emits a per-transition trace certificate and a trusted Lean checker replays it.](/figures/leanguard-intro.svg)

LeanGuard follows an "untrusted producer / trusted checker" workflow:

1. **Days** emits a *per-transition* trace (one row per protocol transition),
   including a deterministic snapshot of post-state.
2. A **Lean 4 checker** canonicalizes the trace. Legacy protocol families
   sort by `(time_ns, event_id)` and reject duplicates; Days AGO checkers
   key on the full `EventKey`, and some require already-strict canonical
   order.
3. The checker **replays** an executable reference model and returns
   `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` with the first counterexample row.

See [/docs/verification/design](/docs/verification/design) for the practical
guide, [/docs/research/design](/docs/research/design) for the design notes,
and [/docs/reference/traces/schema](/docs/reference/traces/schema) for row
conventions. The coverage-guided test-generation loop that builds on these
checkers is summarized in
[/docs/research/test-generation](/docs/research/test-generation).

## Days AGO: proofs and byte identity

The Days AGO claim is structural. Five Lean theorems about the execution
machinery (`lean/DaysExecutor*`) license safe-horizon rounds and
intra-round reordering. The corpus gate then checks every backend against
them: the CPU pool and each compiled device backend must reproduce the
global Scalar reference's complete result byte for byte (device-omitted
diagnostics excluded) on every corpus fixture.

The device arc that made this practical at scale shipped in this tree:
planner equality across backends, persistent arenas, per-entity capacity
derivation with refuse-or-run faults and bounded retry, ring and vector
sizing, compact readback (only live regions return), and an opt-in capacity
warm start that affects sizing only. The largest committed fixture runs
**262,144 closed-loop TCP flows**
(`configs/benchmarks/lookahead/rq9_frontier_closed_k32.toml`).

The tree also ships the TCP corpus byte-identity gate
(`validation/tests/tcp_corpora.rs`), CUDA upload provisioning for
unified-memory hosts, strict legacy configuration validation, and the
TOML `csv_logging` switch for legacy runs.

## Runnable benchmarks

The committed E5 fixture family is the recommended runnable benchmark set:
8,192 byte-terminated TCP flows on a k=32 fat tree, with a Reno primary
(`q200`), a CUBIC variant, and a `q256` backup. See
[Examples](/docs/examples#e5-wide-tcp). Measured run times are
machine-qualified and live in the project's evidence archive, not in these
pages.

## Measured negatives, kept on the record

Two device optimizations were implemented, measured in paired A/B campaigns,
and **not merged**. They are recorded here because the method is the point:

- **Pinned host staging for CUDA uploads**: exactness passed; process wall
  time regressed materially on the discrete-GPU host. Verdict: hold.
- **Structure-of-arrays drain layout**: all seven identity anchors byte-exact;
  slower on the E5 and every E6 device clock in the paired A/B (one process
  wall at parity). Verdict: the shipped layout stands.

## External campaign evidence

The E6 UDP/CBR fixture family is committed in this tree and runnable. Its
multi-engine comparison campaigns (against sequential and MPI ns-3, Unison,
GeDES, and the legacy engine) remain external campaign evidence, recorded in
the project's evidence archive with machine-qualified results. Results
quoted from those campaigns carry their machine and clock definitions at the
citation site.
